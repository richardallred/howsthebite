              Fishing questions, answered

              What catch-rate studies say, and how the score uses it.

              When is the best time of day to go fishing?

              Around sunset, then early morning. In a 13-year survey of every angler on Escanaba Lake, Wisconsin, the predicted chance of a successful walleye trip was about 47% at dusk, 34% at dawn and 26% by day, and it fell as the sun got brighter. An overcast day fishes better than a bright one. In salt water, moving tide matters as much as the light, and shore anglers in a Mississippi survey caught about as well at night as by day. The forecast above works out the best times for today and the next two days wherever you are.

              Sources: Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021 · Mississippi shore creel survey

              What water temperature is best for fishing?

              It depends on the fish. Roughly: largemouth bass and panfish 69–80°F, walleye and pike 62–72°F, trout and salmon 50–64°F. Trout feed poorly once the water passes the mid-60s; Idaho Fish and Game reported catch rates 77% lower above 73°F than below 66°F, and trout caught in warm water are more likely to die after release. In salt water: snook and tarpon 74–86°F, redfish and seatrout 65–82°F, striped bass and bluefish 55–70°F. Water temperature carries the most weight in the score.

              Sources: Idaho Fish and Game · EPA temperature criteria · Salt Water Sportsman temperature guide

              Does barometric pressure affect fishing?

              Less than its reputation suggests. In the three angling datasets that tested it (walleye and muskie in Wisconsin, largemouth bass in Michigan and Illinois), pressure was not a significant predictor of catch. The slow bite after a cold front is widely reported but has barely been measured, so the score gives the pressure trend 5 points out of 100 and the reading itself none.

              Sources: Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021 · Mell 1962 (Michigan bass) · Burkett 1981 (Illinois bass)

              Does the moon affect fishing?

              A little. Across 340,000 muskie records the moon's effect was about 5% overall, and a seven-year trout study found none. Fishing while the moon was overhead or underfoot (the "major periods" of solunar tables) did raise trip success in the Escanaba Lake walleye data. The moon is worth 8 points of the freshwater score.

              Sources: Vinson & Angradi 2014 · Quigley et al. 2023 (trout) · Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021

              Is wind good or bad for fishing?

              Wind speed made no difference to walleye catches on Escanaba Lake, and wind direction and waves made none to bass in a Michigan study, while muskie catches fell about 40% at 20 mph compared with calm. A light breeze of 5–12 mph scores best here; points fall away above 15 mph and are gone at 30, mostly because of boat control, casting and safety.

              Sources: Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021 · Mell 1962 (Michigan bass)

              Is fishing good in the rain?

              Rain itself was not a significant factor for walleye or muskie in the Escanaba Lake study, and the cloud that comes with it cuts the light, which helps. Lightning is another matter: thunderstorms, or a severe-weather warning, hold the score at 15 or below.

              Sources: Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021

              Is an incoming or outgoing tide better for fishing?

              Moving water matters more than its direction. Tagged summer flounder and red drum move with the tide, but which stage is best varies by species and place. The inshore score gives full marks at mid-tide and 40% at slack water, and the tides table shows the next highs and lows from NOAA.

              Sources: summer flounder tagging · Dresser & Kneib 2007 (red drum)

              How does river flow affect fishing?

              High water for the time of year hurts. Wisconsin guide logs showed smallmouth catch rates falling as river discharge rose, while whether the river was rising or falling over three days made no difference. The score compares the nearest USGS gauge with its own record for the date: normal flow scores best and a flood scores almost nothing.

              Sources: Wisconsin DNR guide reports

              How accurate is the fishing score?

              It rates conditions, not your odds. Where catch rates have been measured, the angler's skill and bait outweighed every weather factor, so read the score as "is this a good time to be out". It is built from public data: National Weather Service observations, forecasts and alerts, USGS water gauges, NOAA tide predictions and satellite sea temperatures.

              Sources: Shaw, Renik & Sass 2021 · Wisconsin DNR on live and artificial bait
